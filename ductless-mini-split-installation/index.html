Think about the spaces your central system has always struggled with: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs hot all summer, or an older Longview home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the spaces a ductless mini split was made for. Nothing travels through leaky ductwork, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a three-inch hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor varies its speed to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it works as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Many disappointing mini splits were doomed on paper, not on the wall. The most common error is oversizing: a head too big for its room cools in a hurry, shuts off before it pulls out the humidity, and hunts all day. Longview HVAC sizes every zone to its real load instead. On multi-zone systems we also check that the outdoor unit's capacity is balanced across the heads it feeds and that every line set stays within the manufacturer's length and lift limits. Then comes placement. We walk the Longview home with you, weigh wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor-console options, and put each head where it moves air well and still looks right, with outdoor line sets dressed in line-hide rather than left bare.

On installation day, the small steps that customers never see are the ones we never rush. Every flare is formed carefully, torqued to spec, and leak-checked, because a leaking flare joint is the number one way mini splits fail. The line set gets a nitrogen pressure test, then a deep vacuum confirmed on a micron gauge, before refrigerant ever moves. We make sure condensate drains with proper fall or a dependable pump, trim the charge for the actual run length, and test each zone in heating and cooling. The Benefits of Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion creates a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing room-temperature air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors tolerate poorly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall stains a wall the first humid week.

Sizing is even less forgiving with ductless than with central air. The smallest heads on the market are already more than many bedrooms need, so picking one size too big guarantees short cycles, sticky humidity, and a room that never settles. That is why our experienced technicians size every zone honestly, then back it up with disciplined install work: each flare torqued and leak-tested, the circuit pressure-tested and evacuated to a measured vacuum, charge adjusted for the line length, and every head commissioned in heating and cooling. Handling refrigerant is federally regulated, and the unit needs a correctly sized circuit and disconnect. Done this way, a mini split runs quiet and efficient for a decade and a half with nothing to report.

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Choose a setpoint and leave it alone, since inverters run best without constant on and off switching
  • Schedule service once a year so charge, flares, and drainage get verified before small issues grow
  • Glance at the condensate drain outlet in summer to confirm it is actually dripping
  • In winter, expect defrost pauses and light steam from the outdoor unit; solid ice means call us
  • Have the indoor blower wheel and coil professionally cleaned before they grow anything
  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
  • Clean the washable filter screens in each head about once a month, since they clog faster than furnace filters
